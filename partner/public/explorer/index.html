Coverage, distance and volume

Area, towns, industrial areas, indicative daily volume, dealers and distributors needed, and kilometre distances for every district, cluster, circle and master circle. Pick a phase and a starting point; click a column heading to sort.

How these numbers are made. Area: each district's drawn outline, scaled so the 34 districts add up to Gujarat's 1,96,024 km² (about 5% off per district). Distance: straight line between map points, scaled the same way; road km is straight line × 1.3, drive time assumes about 45 km/h. Distance origin defaults to the Vadodara plant, taken as Vadodara city. Volume: the phase's litres a day (500, 1,000 or 2,000) shared across districts by industrial weight (high = 5, medium = 3, emerging = 1, plus 2 per district for retail and institutional demand); indicative, not measured sales. Dealers needed = 2 per district + 1 per high-volume area + 1 per two medium areas + 1 per full 8,000 km²; distributors = 1 per district. This gives about 110 dealers for Gujarat against the roughly 100 in the rollout plan. Industrial-area volumes are a share of their district's volume. Validate everything against GIDC estate data and your own orders.

Buyers and reach

How many people it takes to sell a daily volume. Set a daily target and how much one person uses; see the regular users needed, the people to reach, and the packs a day, by district, cluster and industrial area.

How to read this. Regular users = litres a day × 1,000 ÷ ml per person per day. This is steady state: users who keep being resupplied, not new buyers every day. People to reach = regular users ÷ the share of reached people who become regular users (an assumption you can change). The daily volume is shared across districts by industrial weight (high = 5, medium = 3, emerging = 1, plus 2 per district for retail and institutional use), the same rule as the Coverage data tab; an industrial area gets its weight's share of its district. The volume is split between bottles and sachets by the share you set (25% sachets, 10 ml each, to start with; both are assumptions). Industrial-area users and retail and institutional users add up to the district total. These are planning estimates, not measured demand.

India planner

Take the Gujarat approach to the whole country. Gujarat starts with your plan; every other state starts open. Click districts to select them, make clusters, give clusters to circles and circles to master circles, or let Auto-build group a whole state in one click.

About the data. District outlines come from an open dataset (mostly Census 2011 era, so some states show fewer districts than today, for example Andhra Pradesh); Gujarat uses the 34 districts in your plan. Outlines are simplified and not an official map. Area is computed from the outlines (Gujarat uses the same areas as the Coverage data tab). Distances are straight lines between district centres, with road km about 1.3 times that, measured from the Vadodara plant. Volume is shared across districts in clusters by weight points: Gujarat districts use the industrial weights from the Coverage data tab; districts elsewhere get the default points you set (2) until industrial data is added for them. Bottle, sachet, user and reach figures use the same rules as the Buyers and reach tab. Everything is a planning estimate. Changes are saved in this browser only. Gujarat groupings made in the Cluster editor can be brought in with Import Gujarat edits.

Cluster editor

Try a different grouping. Move districts between clusters, set each cluster's circle and master circle, and watch area, volume, dealers and distances update. The plan is 2 to 3 districts per cluster, 3 clusters per circle and 2 to 3 circles per master circle.

Edits are saved in this browser only and do not change the other tabs. Use Copy table or Copy JSON to share a grouping. Dealer, volume and distance figures use the same rules as the Coverage data tab (Phase 3, Vadodara plant). The hub is the district with the most industry unless you pick another.

Market potential

For every district, cluster, circle and master circle: its area, how many people live there, how many of them can buy the product, and the buying volume that adds up to in litres a day. Switch between your Gujarat plan and the groupings you make in the India planner.

How these numbers are made. Population: Census 2011 district counts, grown at the yearly rate you set (1.1% to begin with) to the year you pick. Districts formed after 2011 (for example Morbi, Botad, Aravalli in Gujarat) have no census of their own, so their population is cut out of the parent district or districts by area or by the official split; those rows are marked with ≈. Eligible people = the share of the population who use hand wash on their own (80% to begin with, roughly everyone old enough). People who can buy = eligible people × a buy rate, set higher for urban people (30%) than rural people (10%) and blended by each district's urban share of households in Census 2011. Buying volume = people who can buy × ml per buyer a day (10 ml). Area for Gujarat matches the Coverage data tab; elsewhere it comes from the simplified district outlines. These are planning estimates to size territories and compare areas, not forecasts of sales; replace the assumptions with your own once you have real sell-through.
